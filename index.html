1 · Waveform

One frame of period samples (default 1024) is loaded into the transmit buffer and played cyclically, so the radar emits an endless, exactly periodic pulse train. TX1 carries a linear-FM up-chirp at sample 0; TX2 carries a down-chirp at period/2. Each chirp is pulse samples long (default 256), sweeps BW/fs × fs (default 0.8 × 61.44 = 49 MHz) and has a 15 % raised-cosine (Tukey) edge taper so it does not splatter. Amplitude is half of DAC full scale. In live mode only the selected transmitter is on.

Pulse repetition interval = period / fs = 16.7 µs. Transmit and receive run at the same time (the AD9361 is full duplex), so there is no blind range.

2 · Radio

AD9361, 2 TX + 2 RX, sharing one sample clock and one local-oscillator setting (default 5.8 GHz). RF bandwidth is set to 0.9 × fs (max 56 MHz). Receive gain is manual (no AGC), because AGC would modulate the very amplitude and phase the radar measures. Converters are 12-bit; all levels here are relative to converter full scale (dBFS).

3 · Capture and folding

Each capture is K contiguous periods from both receivers (K = 8 on the fast link, 4 on debug serial). Because the transmit train is exactly periodic and shares the receive clock, the capture is cut into K periods and averaged coherently: signal adds in voltage, noise in power, for 10·log10(K) dB of gain. The capture starts at an arbitrary point in the period; step 5 resolves that.

4 · Pulse compression

The averaged period is circularly cross-correlated with the transmitted chirp in the frequency domain: h = IFFT( FFT(rx) · conj(FFT(ref)) · W ) / mean(|FFT(ref)|² · W). This gives the delay profile (channel impulse response) with 10·log10(pulse) = 24 dB of processing gain. W is a Hamming weighting across the chirp band: it lowers range sidelobes from −13 dB to about −30 dB at the cost of a 1.4× wider mainlobe. Without it, sidelobes of the strong direct leakage would bury nearby echoes. The normalisation makes h read directly as path gain.

5 · Delay anchoring

The strongest peak of the profile is the direct antenna-to-antenna leakage. Its index is taken as delay bin 0 and bins −4…+19 are kept. One bin = c / (2·fs) = 2.44 m of range at 61.44 Msps; resolution is c / (2·BW) ≈ 3 m, widened to ≈ 4.3 m by the Hamming weighting. Phase is not normalised to the leakage: a person in the room sits in the same bin as the leakage, and normalising would erase them. This works because transmit and receive are phase-locked (measured capture-to-capture phase stability ≈ 0.5°).

6 · Quality metrics (sections 1, 2 and the tiles)

  • Digital loopback: the AD9361 loops DAC-port samples to the ADC port; received samples are compared with the sent ones (≤ 1 LSB passes).
  • ADC peak / clipped %: largest I or Q sample; clipping must be 0.
  • Raw SNR: pulse power over the period-to-period variance. Compressed SNR adds the compression and averaging gain.
  • Peak sidelobe: highest lobe outside the mainlobe nulls within ±40 bins, relative to the peak. Mainlobe: −3 dB width.
  • Phase jitter / amplitude ripple: spread of the per-period complex peak inside one capture.

7 · Slow time

Every capture yields one complex delay profile, time-stamped on arrival. The last N profiles (N = “Frames per Doppler window”) form the slow-time buffer of each receiver. Host timing is not perfectly regular, so before any FFT the buffer is linearly re-sampled onto a uniform grid; the effective pulse-repetition frequency (PRF) is the measured mean frame rate (≈ 100–300 Hz over USB). Maximum unaliased speed = PRF · λ / 4.

8 · Static-scene removal

A background profile is tracked with an exponential average (time constant ≈ 3 s) and subtracted: this is the “static scene removed” plot. For Doppler processing the slow-time mean of each bin is subtracted instead.

9 · Delay–Doppler map

Each delay bin’s slow-time series is Hann-windowed and Fourier transformed. Doppler frequency f maps to radial velocity v = f · λ / 2 (λ = 51.7 mm at 5.8 GHz); positive = approaching. Velocity resolution = λ · PRF / (2·N).

10 · Optional slow-time and Doppler filters

All are off-able; with everything off the plots show the raw slow-time spectrum. They act on the uniformly re-sampled slow-time buffer of each delay bin, in this order:

  • DC removal (mean): subtracts the slow-time average of each delay bin, i.e. the static scene and the leakage. With it off, the zero-Doppler line dominates the map.
  • Savitzky–Golay: a least-squares polynomial of the chosen order is fitted in a sliding window of frames, on I and Q separately. Detrend subtracts the fit, removing drift and slow movers while leaving fast motion almost untouched and without the ringing of a sharp filter; smooth keeps the fit, which suppresses fast components and noise. Its −3 dB point is roughly (order + 1) · PRF / (3.2 · window).
  • High-pass: zero-phase 4th-order Butterworth (applied forwards and backwards) at the chosen cutoff. Sharper than Savitzky–Golay detrending, but with edge transients at the ends of the buffer.
  • FFT window: Hann (default), Hamming, Blackman or rectangular taper before the Doppler FFT. Blackman has the lowest leakage from the zero-Doppler line, rectangular the finest resolution.
  • Doppler keep above / below: band limits applied to the spectrum itself with smooth 4th-order edges, |H|² = 1 / (1 + (f_lo/f)⁸) and 1 / (1 + (f/f_hi)⁸). 0 disables an edge. 1 Hz of Doppler is 25.8 mm/s.

The plots show exactly what these filters leave; nothing is blanked or filled. Independently of the filters, detection, motion energy and angle ignore zero Doppler and two bins either side.

11 · Detection, motion energy, displacement

  • Doppler peak: strongest cell in delay bins −1…+3 away from zero Doppler, after the selected filters; its SNR is measured against the median along Doppler in the same delay bin. A circle is drawn above 18 dB.
  • Motion energy: total filtered Doppler power away from zero in those bins, relative to its own 20th-percentile level over the last ≈ 20 s. Above 10 dB counts as motion. The quiet level is learned because it is set by leakage jitter, not thermal noise.
  • Displacement: phase of (profile − background) in the most active near bin, unwrapped capture by capture: Δr = −Δφ · λ / (4π), i.e. 25.8 mm per full turn. It only integrates while motion energy is above 6 dB and the return is above twice the quiet level; otherwise residual noise would random-walk. This trace is not Doppler-filtered.

12 · Angle from the two receivers

For each Doppler bin the cross-spectrum S₁₂ = Σ X₁ · conj(X₂) is summed over the near delay bins. Working in the Doppler domain means only moving reflectors contribute; the static scene and the leakage are excluded. S₁₂ is averaged over a few looks (≈ 0.3 s) and only Doppler bins standing at least 6 dB above the median are used, so empty bins do not dilute the estimate. The phase of the summed S₁₂ is the inter-receiver phase difference Δφ, and θ = asin( (Δφ − offset) · λ / (2π · d) ), with d the receive-antenna spacing. Coherence = |ΣS₁₂| / √(ΣP₁ · ΣP₂): near 1 means one dominant direction; low means several movers or multipath, and the angle should not be trusted.

Limits: (a) the two receive chains have an unknown fixed phase offset, so move straight in front of the antennas and press Set current direction as 0°; (b) at 2-inch spacing d ≈ 0.98 λ, so angles are unambiguous only within about ±31° and wrap beyond that; (c) two receivers resolve one angle, in the plane containing both antennas, for one dominant mover; (d) the sign convention (+ = towards RX1) should be checked once by moving to a known side.

0 · Radio

Connect, then set the waveform. “Auto” uses the fast libiio link if the board’s data port is reachable and otherwise falls back to the slow debug-serial path.

1 · Are the samples reaching the transmitter? (digital loopback)

Loops the DAC-port samples straight back into the ADC port inside the AD9361, with no RF involved. The received pulse must equal the sent one to the bit.

2 · Are the pulses going out and coming back? (over the air)

Each transmitter fires alone; both receivers listen. With antennas 2″ apart the direct antenna-to-antenna leakage is the reference signal: its envelope, spectrum and compressed shape show transmit and receive quality.

3 · Live: pulse quality, delay–Doppler, motion

Continuous pulse train on one transmitter. Each capture is compressed to a range profile anchored on the direct path; static returns are subtracted, and what remains is tracked over slow time.
